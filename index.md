<h1>Zubeyir Salis</h1>
<p class="role">Junior Professor, University of Montpellier</p>
<p class="lead">I study how rheumatic diseases develop and change over time, and how health data can help us predict outcomes and evaluate treatments.</p>
<p>My research combines epidemiology, machine learning and longitudinal data analysis. I work with clinical cohorts, international registries and French national health data, with a particular focus on osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis.</p>

## Research themes

<div class="themes">
<article class="theme"><h3><a href="research.html#prediction">Prediction in rheumatology</a></h3><p>Predicting disease onset, remission and progression, and assessing whether models work in new populations.</p></article>
<article class="theme"><h3><a href="research.html#osteoarthritis">Osteoarthritis across time and joints</a></h3><p>Understanding changes in pain, joint structure and function, and their relationships with body weight.</p></article>
<article class="theme"><h3><a href="research.html#environment">Environment, metabolism and the microbiome</a></h3><p>Studying environmental exposures and biological factors that may contribute to rheumatic disease.</p></article>
<article class="theme"><h3><a href="research.html#treatments">Treatment outcomes and population health</a></h3><p>Using routine health data to investigate treatment effects, safety and multimorbidity.</p></article>
<article class="theme"><h3><a href="research.html#digital-twins">Digital twins and clinical AI</a></h3><p>Developing and evaluating approaches that connect patient data, computational models and clinical questions.</p></article>
</div>
